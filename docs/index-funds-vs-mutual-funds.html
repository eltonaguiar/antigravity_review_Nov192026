Index funds vs active mutual funds — the actual numbers

Four low-cost index funds against four large active mutual funds, at 1, 3, 5 and 10 years, using total-return NAVs (net of each fund's own expenses). Plus the fee-drag arithmetic at 5/10/20/30 years.

ELI5: Two buckets hold the same kind of stocks. One charges ~0.00–0.04% a year; the other charges ~0.57–0.61%. Over ten years the cheap bucket kept more of the return in all four match-ups. Over one year it was mixed — active funds can win a sprint. Fees are the part that never takes a year off.

1. The claim, with its assumptions on the table

The site's fee table says an active mutual fund costs $18,520 per $100,000 over ten years, an index mutual fund $4,520, and an index ETF $1,050 (17.6× / 4.3×). Those are all-in cost assumptions, not the expense ratios of the funds below: 1.85%/yr for active (including 12b-1/advice), 0.45%/yr for an average index mutual fund, 0.105%/yr for an ETF.

Important correction: the cheapest index mutual funds are not 4.3× an ETF. FXAIX charges 0.015% and VFIAX/VTSAX charge 0.04% — as cheap as, or cheaper than, many ETFs. The 4.3× figure is the average index mutual fund, not the best ones.

4. What the data actually says

  • 10 years: every active fund lagged FXAIX (13.96%/yr): AGTHX −0.32 pp, ANCFX −0.89 pp, AIVSX −1.51 pp, AWSHX −1.54 pp per year.
  • 5 years: averages were close (index 9.76% vs active 10.01%), but AGTHX was far behind at 6.94%; one active (ANCFX 11.39%) beat the best index.
  • 3 years: active averaged 16.21% vs index 16.10% — a statistical tie; 3 of 4 actives still trailed the best index.
  • 1 year: mixed. Index averaged +2.06%, active −0.35%; but AWSHX (+3.96%) and ANCFX (+2.23%) beat FXAIX (+2.04%). A single year proves nothing either way.
  • The persistent part is cost: 0.57–0.61% vs 0.00–0.04% is charged every year, in every window, win or lose.

Which index funds would beat those mutual funds? Over ten years, all four of FXAIX (0.015%), VFIAX (0.04%), VTSAX (0.04%) and FZROX (0.00%, shorter history) are cheaper than AGTHX, AIVSX, ANCFX and AWSHX (0.57–0.61%). On this window, the S&P 500 index funds beat every one of the four active funds over 10 years; over 1 year, two of the four active funds won. That is the honest version.

5. Caveats — read before quoting this

  • Survivorship: these are four large, surviving active funds. Funds that closed or merged are not in the sample, which flatters active management.
  • Past performance: a 10-year win is evidence about costs and this window, not a guarantee for the next ten years.
  • Loads and advice fees: the table uses expense ratios only. Sales loads, platform fees and advisor fees make active worse, not better.
  • Taxes and account type: not modelled. In Canada, US-listed ETFs belong in an RRSP; CAD-listed wrappers (VFV/XEQT) avoid FX and withholding friction in a TFSA.
