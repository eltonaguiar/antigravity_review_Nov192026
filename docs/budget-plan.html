The answer, in one line

At every budget, the defensible allocation to the picks is zero today. Not because the picks lost once, but because the measured rate loses 10% of the account in 25 trades — and that is true at $100 and at $30,000 alike. Capital size does not change the sign of an expectation.

25 trades. At the measured rate, 25 trades costs 10% of the account — regardless of whether the account holds $100 or $30,000. That is the single most important number on this page: the loss is proportional, so a bigger budget does not buy more room, it buys a bigger loss.

Why more money does not fix it

Size changes three things and none of them is the sign of the expectation.

  • Cost is not the problem at small size. A round turn on a $24 position costs about 6 cents. The cost of the strategy is not its fees, it is that it loses 1.41% before fees.
  • Size does not reduce variance in proportion. Risking 1% per trade scales linearly, so expected loss scales linearly too. A $30,000 account funding this loses about $121 per pick.
  • Capital was never the binding constraint. The binding constraint is n: deciding whether the picks work needs about 4,493 observations at 21 days, and the book holds 284. That gap is closed by time, not money — and paper trading closes it at zero cost.

What would change the answer

When a cohort passes, a satellite of 5–10% of the budget is defensible, sized so that its total drawdown halts at 10% of the whole account. Until then the satellite is 0%.
