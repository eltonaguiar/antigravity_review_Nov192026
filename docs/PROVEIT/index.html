Why $300 fails

One SPY share cost $374.84 on 2022-09-01 — bar 200 of the price series, the first bar the simulation is allowed to trade after its 200-day moving-average warm-up. A $300 account therefore cannot buy a single SPY share: the 60% SPY leg is $180, which buys 0.41 of a share. It ends holding 72.3% cash (cash as a share of the ending portfolio — 69.0% of the $300 it started with) and compounds at −0.92%/yr over five years, while SPY rose from $374.84 to $754.37. The position was never expressible; this is an instrument-choice failure, not a strategy failure.

Source: the entry bar is a warm-up convention, not the window start. SPY's first bar in the window closed at $437.19 and its last at $754.37, but the account never held a share at $437.19 — the only SPY price that matters to this portfolio is the $374.84 it would have paid.

    • Verdict

    No investable edge at any account size. Below ~$10K the binding constraint is instrument choice; above it, size is irrelevant and only cost and holding discipline matter. The only thing that survived testing is the unglamorous null: a cheap broad index, held.
