# PROVEIT — can the trading philosophy actually be proven?

**Kilo Code · 2026-11-24 · PAPER ONLY · NOT INVESTMENT ADVICE**

This folder is a self-contained, reproducible attempt to *prove* (or fail to prove) a
trading philosophy across asset classes, for four account sizes, with the costs and
fees stated. Everything is stdlib Python with fixed seeds, so every number below can
be regenerated by anyone.

The honest one-line answer: **no.** With the edge this programme has actually
measured, **no** portfolio, bracket, or account size produces a return interval
that clears the 4.32% T-bill hurdle. What *can* be shown with math is a different
and still useful thing: **which choices cost you money and which do not**, and that
is what this folder proves.

---

## 1. The philosophy, stated as rules

These are the rules the simulations apply. They are deliberately boring.

| Decision | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Universe** | Only classes with a *measured* expectancy. FUTURES is excluded (n=4). | A verdict on 4 trades would be fabricated. |
| **Entry** | A rule is eligible only if its measured expectancy CI excludes zero on the *positive* side. | A point estimate above zero is not an edge. |
| **Sizing** | Fixed-fractional per sleeve; weight by capital, not by confidence. | Confidence-weighted sizing is how one bad class eats the book. |
| **Exit** | Hold cap of one year unless a bracket is set. | "Hold forever and hope" is not a strategy; it is an unmeasured bet. |
| **Bracket (TP/SL)** | Optional, and reported *separately* from the headline. | A bracket reshapes the return distribution; it cannot create edge (bracket identity). |
| **Cash floor** | 5–40% depending on account size, earning the 4.32% hurdle. | For small accounts cash is often the best risk-adjusted sleeve. |
| **Hurdle** | 4.32% T-bill, compared as `(1+a)/(1+h)-1`, never `a-h`. | The difference is exactly what flipped the bond test from +84bp to −91.58bp. |
| **Verdict** | PROVEN only if the *whole* CI is above the hurdle. CI spanning 0 = not proven. | The programme's own convention. |

## 2. Fees used, and why they matter

Costs are charged **both legs**, in basis points of notional (see `docs/inputs.json`):

| Profile | Round-turn | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| `programme_sweep` | **26 bp** | The figure the 44-cell sweep actually charged (`30_COST_STRUCTURE_2026-11-24/COST_VS_SIGNAL.md`) |
| `retail_crypto` | 40 bp | MODELED: 0.1% taker/side ×2 + slippage |
| `retail_stock` | 5 bp | MODELED: 0 commission + ~2.5bp spread/side |
| `retail_etf` | 4 bp | MODELED |
| `retail_forex` | 3 bp | MODELED: spread-only broker |
| `retail_fund` | 0 bp + 4 bp/yr expense | VTSAX 0.04% |

Commission sensitivity, assuming a 5% gross return and 20 round trips a year:

| Account | $0/trade | $1/trade | $5/trade | $5 drag |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $300 | $331 | $311 | $229 | **34.1%** |
| $500 | $552 | $531 | $449 | **20.5%** |
| $2,000 | $2,208 | $2,187 | $2,105 | 5.1% |
| $10,000 | $11,038 | $11,017 | $10,935 | 1.0% |

**The single most defensible finding here:** at $300–500, a $5 commission is not a
rounding error, it is a third to a fifth of your capital per year. Any active
philosophy is mathematically unsuited to a small account at full-service
commissions. This is not a matter of skill.

## 4. Scenario endorsements

Generated by `sim/proveit.py` (`results/run_2026-11-24T0323Z_baseline.json`),
1,000 Monte Carlo books, retail costs, one year:

| Account | Allocation | Median APR | CI95 | Excess vs hurdle | Verdict |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| **$300** | 40% cash / 40% bond / 20% ETF | −0.40% | [−6.96%, +9.93%] | −4.52% | INCONCLUSIVE |
| **$500** | 20% cash / 30% bond / 35% ETF / 15% equity | +2.17% | [−12.01%, +24.04%] | −2.06% | INCONCLUSIVE |
| **$2,000** | 10% cash / 20% bond / 40% ETF / 25% equity / 5% crypto | +1.10% | [−17.89%, +33.27%] | −3.08% | INCONCLUSIVE |
| **$10,000** | 5% cash / 10% bond / 45% ETF / 30% equity / 10% crypto | −0.10% | [−23.26%, +38.91%] | −4.24% | INCONCLUSIVE |

None is endorsed. Each has a median *below* the hurdle and a CI far too wide to say
otherwise. The wider the account, the wider the interval, because more capital is
exposed to the negative-expectancy classes.

**Why bigger accounts do worse, not better:** in the $10,000 book the crypto sleeve
($1,000 at 10%) ends at $326 over 200 trades. A −0.29%/trade edge compounded over
200 trades is a near-total loss. Diversification does not fix a negative edge; it
just spreads it.

## 5. Is the philosophy better than buying an index?

`sim/compare.py` (`results/compare_2026-11-24.txt`), $10,000, retail costs:

| Portfolio | Median APR | CI95 | Beats hurdle | Loses money |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| cash only (T-bill) | +4.32% | [4.32%, 4.32%] | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| index ETF only | −25.90% | [−49.95%, +9.22%] | 4.2% | 93.5% |
| equity only | −9.45% | [−50.90%, +69.24%] | 34.5% | 61.4% |
| crypto only | −68.69% | [−94.50%, +49.22%] | 6.5% | 92.6% |
| diversified | −15.66% | [−32.71%, +7.48%] | 4.0% | 91.2% |

Note the uncomfortable row: **index-only shows −25.90%** because this programme's
*measured* ETF expectancy is −0.0062%/trade at 26bp, and a 200-trade year
compounds that. That is a property of the picks this system generates, **not** a
forecast about markets: the long-run index benchmark (§2, MODELED ~7%/yr real) is
a different thing from this programme's measured ETF selection. Both are reported
because conflating them is exactly the error this programme keeps finding.

**The only row that beats the hurdle is cash.** That is the verdict.

## 6. Parameter sweep: is anything robust, or is it noise?

`sim/sweep.py` (`results/sweep_2026-11-24.txt`), 24 cells (4 accounts × 3 brackets ×
2 costs):

- **PROVEN in the unbracketed headline: 0.** KILLED: 5. INCONCLUSIVE: 19.
- Bracketed variants do reach PROVEN_EDGE, but **all of them are the same bracket
  (tp2/sl1)**. That is a property of one rule's truncation, not five independent
  discoveries. With 24 trials at 5%, ~1.2 false positives are expected by chance.
- Robustness (share of a scenario's 6 variations beating the hurdle on the median):
  $300 0/6, $500 1/6, $2,000 2/6, $10,000 2/6. Nothing is consistent.

## 7. What would change this answer

1. **A class whose expectancy CI excludes zero on the positive side.** None does today.
2. **Cheaper execution.** The cost-vs-signal analysis says this is a *signal* problem:
   median gross bp/trade is −1.51, so even zero cost does not rescue most cells.
3. **Real forward bars.** The contest round is open with 4 of 24 symbols covered;
   per-class forward returns are BLOCKED until the rest have bars.
4. **A cell surviving deflated Sharpe.** One reached permutation p=0.001 and died at
   DSR 0.0031.

## 8. How to reproduce

```bash
python3 sim/proveit.py --sims 1000 --out results/run_<stamp>.json
python3 sim/sweep.py
python3 sim/compare.py
python3 TRACKING/tracker.py --check      # see TRACKING/README.md
```

Every run writes a date-time stamped file under `results/`. The tracker in
`TRACKING/` is how later runs get appended and compared rather than overwritten.
