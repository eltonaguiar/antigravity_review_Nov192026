Reverse engineering daily or weekly top gainers across asset classes like stocks, crypto, mutual funds, or forex to enable reliable signals with target profit (TP) and stop loss (SL) can be a complex but systematic endeavor. Here's a structured approach to tackle this problem:

1. Define the Objective and Scope
Goal: Identify predictable patterns or signals that precede strong daily/weekly top gains, enabling actionable trade ideas with defined TP and SL.
Asset Classes: Stocks, cryptocurrencies, mutual funds, forex.
Outputs: Buy/sell signals, TP/SL levels, confidence or probability scores.
Time horizon: Daily or weekly signals.
2. Data Collection & Preparation
Historical Price Data: OHLC (open/high/low/close), volume, order book (for crypto), bid/ask spreads (for forex).
Fundamental Data: Earnings, sector classification (stocks), token metrics (crypto), interest rates (forex).
Alternative Data: Social sentiment, news events, on-chain data (crypto), fund flows (mutual funds).
Market Regimes: Volatility indices, macroeconomic conditions.

Key considerations:

Ensure data cleanliness and synchronization across assets and timezones.
Adjust stock data for splits/dividends and crypto for forks or token changes.
Frequency: Use intraday data if available for finer signals; otherwise daily for weekly aggregates.
3. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Distribution of returns: Examine return profiles for each asset class and individual symbols.
Volatility and volume patterns: Are top gainers associated with volume spikes or high volatility periods?
Cross-asset comparisons: Which asset classes tend to have more concentrated top gainers or more consistent patterns?
Symbol-specific behavior: Do certain stocks or crypto tokens appear frequently as top gainers or losers? How do their historical return profiles look?
Direction bias: Are long or short opportunities more predictable per asset/symbol?
4. Hypotheses Formulation
Some assets or sectors might be inherently more predictable due to liquidity, market structure, or information symmetry.
Certain directional bets (long vs short) might have higher hit rates.
Technical setups (moving average crossovers, momentum spikes), fundamental triggers (earnings surprises), or sentiment shifts could precede big moves.
TP and SL can be derived from historical average moves, ATR (average true range)-based volatility bands, or machine-learned patterns.
5. Modeling & Signal Generation
Step 1: Define potential features
Technical indicators: Moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, momentum.
Volume-based indicators: Volume spikes, OBV, VWAP deviations.
Sentiment scores: News sentiment, social media sentiment (especially in crypto).
On-chain metrics: For crypto, active addresses, transaction volumes.
Fundamental triggers: Earnings surprises, upgrades/downgrades, economic releases.
Price action features: Gaps, candlestick patterns.
Market regime indicators: Volatility index levels, interest rates.
Step 2: Labeling the dataset
Label each day/week per asset as:
Top gainer (e.g., top 10% or top N performing assets in that period).
Not top gainer (the rest).
Optionally, separate by direction (long = top gainers, short = top losers).
Step 3: Model choice and training
Classification models: Logistic regression, random forests, gradient boosting, or deep learning for signal/no-signal classification.
Regression models: Predict future % move or likelihood of reaching TP/SL levels.
Consider time series models (LSTM, Temporal CNNs) for dependency on sequential data.
Evaluate if asset class or particular symbols benefit from transfer learning or asset-specific models.
Step 4: Definition of TP and SL
TP and SL can be set by:
Historical volatility (e.g., 1.5x ATR for TP, 1x ATR for SL).
Statistical quantiles of past winning/losing moves.
Optimized through backtesting to maximize risk-adjusted returns.
6. Back-testing Strategy
Historical simulations: Apply signals on historical data and simulate trades with realistic slippage and commissions.
Walk-forward analysis: Train on past data, test on unseen future periods to assess stability.
Cross-validation: Different folds in time or assets.

Metrics to track:

Hit rate: Percentage of trades achieving TP.
Profit factor: Gross profits divided by gross losses.
Sharpe ratio: Risk-adjusted return.
Max drawdown: Largest peak-to-trough loss.
Expectancy: Average return per trade.
Trade duration: Average holding period.
Confusion matrix: For classification (true positives, false positives).
7. Forward Testing & Live Validation
Deploy models/signals in a simulated real-time environment.
Record actual trade outcomes compared to predictions.
Monitor signal decay or regime shifts that degrade performance.
Include real-world constraints like trading limits or liquidity.
8. Asset Class & Symbol Predictability Analysis
Compare model performance across asset classes (stocks vs crypto vs forex vs mutual funds).
Analyze symbol-level stability: some symbols might show persistent predictability (e.g., large caps or certain cryptos), while others are more random.
Check directional bias: Are long signals more successful than shorts consistently?
9. Proven Approaches & Literature Insights
Momentum Anomaly: Stocks demonstrating momentum effects over months often continue to outperform short term.
Mean reversion: In forex and crypto, short-term mean reversion is sometimes observed.
Volatility Breakouts: Many technical systems use volatility expansion to time entries.
Machine learning: Recent papers show mixed but often improved results with well-engineered features and proper overfitting controls.
Sentiment as leading indicator: Particularly in crypto, social sentiment often precedes price moves.
10. Considerations and Caveats
Overfitting risk: Complex models can fit noise; rigorous validation is mandatory.
Market regime changes: Past performance might not hold in new regimes.
Data snooping bias: Avoid testing too many hypotheses without out-of-sample validation.
Slippage and fees: Especially critical in crypto and forex; can erode small edges.
Liquidity: Some assets are illiquid, making realistic execution difficult.
